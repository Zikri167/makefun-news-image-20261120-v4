One day's world news on a single broadsheet front page: Friday 20 November 2026,
summed up in one 1024×1024 image carrying 24 stories.

## What is on the page

Lead stories (illustrated):

1. Trump, Xi and Putin hold the first-ever trilateral summit in Shenzhen, at APEC, ending with a joint peace declaration.
2. Cyclone Akvan crosses the Odisha coast near Gopalpur at noon, winds 90–100 km/h.

Story grid:

3. APEC adopts the Shenzhen Vision — 21 economies back a trade, AI and data pact.
4. Xi pledges $20m for APEC cooperation.
5. ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya — landmark 9–6 judgment in The Hague.
6. Iran still holds 2,500 missiles, US intelligence counters the destroyed-arsenal claim.
7. Houthi missile hits Riyadh's tallest tower, the Saudi wealth-fund HQ.
8. Tanker hits a mine in the Strait of Hormuz.
9. Europe ready to talk to Russia on ending the Ukraine war.
10. Ballistic missiles hit Kyiv.
11. Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth, 49 years after launch.
12. COP31 climate talks in Türkiye enter their final stretch.
13. Wellington Test, day two: Nicholls 113 not out, New Zealand 243 for 5 against India.
14. Gaza: tanks cross the ceasefire line.
15. Fiji declares a drought disaster.
16. Anthropic valued at $1.5 trillion.
17. World Children's Day (20 November) marked around the world.
18. 300,000 people evacuated from Odisha ahead of Cyclone Akvan.

In brief:

19. Hundreds of rescue teams deployed in Odisha.
20. Akvan is the third Bay of Bengal cyclone in a month.
21. COP31 fossil-fuel wording still split.
22. Tehran arsenal-destroyed claim countered.
23. Shenzhen Vision covers trade, AI and data.
24. World Court vote was 9–6 in The Hague.

The page also carries a schematic world map with a numbered pin for each
story location (plus an arrow for Voyager 1 in deep space) and a
"Day in Numbers" box: 90–100 km/h winds, 300,000+ evacuated, 2,500 missiles,
a 9–6 court vote, 49 years of flight, $1.5tn, 113 not out, 21 economies.

## How it works

`main.py` uses only the Python standard library. The finished page is stored
in the repo as `entry.png`; the program copies it to `/output/entry.png`
(creating `/output` if needed) and checks the copy kept every byte.
The output is deterministic: the same `entry.png` is delivered on every run.
